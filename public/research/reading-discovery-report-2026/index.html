Executive summary

Five findings worth citing

FINDING 01

The catalog is meaningfully bilingual

English represents 68.4% of the catalog and Hindi 31.6%, creating a substantial Hindi-language discovery layer rather than a token translation section.

FINDING 02

Classic literature is a major catalog pillar

Literary fiction and classic-literature categories are among the largest groups, while Hindi novels form one of the largest single category labels.

FINDING 03

Regional Indian literature already has visible depth

The catalog includes at least 100 titles each tagged under Telugu, Malayalam and Kannada regional-literature categories.

FINDING 04

Early search demand is India-heavy

In the latest settled Search Console window used for this report, India generated 2,110 impressions and 18 clicks — roughly 84% of measured impressions and 78% of clicks.

FINDING 05

Hindi literature queries are appearing early

Queries around Kamayani, Maila Anchal, Banbhatt Ki Atmakatha and Atit Ke Chalchitra are already surfacing in Google Search, alongside English nonfiction-summary searches.

Methodology and limitations

Catalog data

Aggregated production Booknomics database snapshot from 11 October 2026. No personal user data is included.

Search data

Google Search Console performance data settled through 6 October 2026. Search data is early-stage and should be treated as directional rather than representative of India as a whole.

Category counts

Counts reflect Booknomics' internal catalog labels. Category names can overlap conceptually and should not be treated as an industry taxonomy.

Author counts

Counts measure representation inside the catalog only. They do not measure readership, sales, influence or popularity.

Important: This is a Booknomics catalog and discovery report — not a national reading survey. Any citation should preserve that distinction.
